# func_80058058

## Verified facts

- The canonical USA body starts at VRAM `0x80058058`, ROM `0x58C58`, and is
  `0x1DFC` (7,676) bytes long. Its terminal `jr ra` is at `0x80059E4C`; the
  delay slot at `0x80059E50` restores the `0x1E0`-byte stack frame.
  `func_80059E54` begins immediately afterward.
- The only direct USA caller is `func_8005B3F0`. It passes the current racer
  actor and HUD mode zero or one after updating that actor's other race HUD
  elements.
- The actor field at `0x84` points to the displayed craft. This routine reads
  its state words at `0x60` and `0x64`, a warning value at `0x218`, six float
  component levels at `0x288`, six component flag words at `0x2A0`, and a
  countdown at `0x2B8`. These offsets agree with the recovered caller and
  neighboring craft/HUD routines.
- The USA body has no computed jump or jump table. All branches and both early
  return paths were followed through their delay slots. Its 45 static helper
  calls cover the freeze-state query, random values, sprite visibility,
  position, scale, color, warning text, and warning sounds.
- The compiled IDO 5.3 candidate has the same 45 call targets in the same
  order. Direct inspection of the stack-passed text arguments established that
  `D_800A59B8[mode]` is the flashing phase and that the final two warning text
  calls use RGB/alpha `(128,128,255,pulse)` and `(64,128,64,pulse)`.
- The equivalent regional routines are JP `func_80058C1C` at ROM `0x5981C`
  (`0x1DB8` bytes) and EU `func_8005CFE4` at ROM `0x5DBE4` (`0x1F34` bytes).
  Their callers make the same three mode-selecting calls, and their prologues,
  terminal delay slots, component loops, and sprite call sequences confirm the
  USA structure. Regional rendering differences were not imported into the
  canonical recovery.

## Behavioral specification

Maintain and draw one player's six-part craft-status display. The routine
opens or closes the display according to craft state, advances its flashing
phase and visibility timers when the game is not frozen, and hides all eight
mode-specific sprites once the display has faded out.

While visible, it derives a color and alpha for each craft component from its
level and flag word. Shutdown and disabled states receive separate palettes;
the ordinary state pulses full, marked, selected, and critical components and
clears an expired transient flag. Front- and rear-half critical components
also change their corresponding arrow colors.

In the ordinary state it draws the applicable marked, low-value, full-level,
or critical warning text and schedules their sounds against shared clocks.
Finally it positions the six component sprites in two columns, applies the
screen-mirror flag, fades their stored colors by display visibility, and draws
the two directional arrows with a squared visibility fade.

## Recovery notes

- `func_8002F054` takes no arguments and returns the global freeze/debug state;
  stray live registers at its call sites are not parameters.
- `D_800A59B8` wraps from zero back toward one and drives flashing text and
  component colors. `D_800A59C0` is the separately integrated display
  visibility used for sprite motion and alpha.
- Component flags are toggled in the canonical order `1, 0, 5, 4, 3, 2`, but
  no helper call occurs between stores. The source keeps this order while using
  arrays for the later scans and rendering pass.
- The local open-source control-flow draft was used only to expose the large
  reducible graph. Helper contracts, field offsets, delay slots, floating-point
  conversions, and every retained call argument were checked against the USA
  assembly; the committed C is independently expressed.

## Status

**Behavior-recovered C, not byte-matching.** IDO 5.3 emits 7,588 bytes versus
the canonical 7,676 and first differs at byte `0x3`. IDO 7.1 emits 7,512 bytes
and also first differs at byte `0x3`. The equal 45-call sequence supports the
behavioral recovery, but the USA split must continue using assembly until an
exact compiler comparison succeeds.
