# func_80088B70

## Boundary

USA VRAM 0x80088B70-0x80088BEC, ROM 0x89770, 124 bytes (31 instructions).
The assigned boundary is correct. The body ends with a return whose delay slot
is empty, preceded by the stack-pointer release; the preceding label
(0x80088B6C) is a single padding instruction and nothing falls through into
this entry. Both direct calls target 0x80088B70 itself, and no branch inside
the body leaves the range. Frame is 0x18 bytes: sixteen bytes of outgoing
argument space plus one slot for the return address. No callee-saved register
is saved or restored.

## Calling convention (the reason this looked hand-written)

The routine reads two callee-saved registers before writing them and destroys
three more without saving them. That is not the ordinary convention and is why
the body was never attempted. It is nevertheless ordinary compiler output: the
translation unit was built one optimisation level above the rest of the ROM,
where the compiler allocates registers across every function of the unit at
once, so a file-local routine whose call sites are all visible takes its
arguments wherever the allocator wanted them and may clobber registers the
caller reloads afterwards. Both call sites make this explicit - each one
materialises the two arguments into the registers the body reads and spills
one further callee-saved register to its own frame immediately before the
call, reloading it immediately after, precisely because the callee destroys it.

Written `static` beside a stand-in caller and compiled at that level, the
recovered source reproduces the body byte for byte on the first attempt, in
all four ROMs.

## Callers

Two direct calls, both inside func_80088BFC (0x80088EB8 and 0x800890E0).
Neither consumes the result, so the routine returns nothing.

## Callees

- func_80090500, twice: once with the constant one, whose result is kept and
  passed back on the second call. This is the interrupt-mask save/restore pair
  that brackets the list surgery.
- func_80088020 (unlink a node from its doubly linked list) once per matching
  node.
- func_80088050 (insert a node after an anchor) once per matching node.

## Globals and access widths

None. Everything is reached through the two pointer arguments.

Object layout used:
- anchor (argument one): a word at offset 0x8 is the head of the list walked;
  the anchor itself is also passed to the insert routine, which uses its word
  at offset 0x0.
- list node: word at 0x0 is the successor, word at 0x8 is a size or count,
  word at 0x10 is the owner tag compared against argument two. Offsets 0x0 and
  0x4 are the list links used by the unlink and insert routines.

## Regional addresses

Japan 0x80089860 (ROM 0x8A460), Europe 0x8008E0C0 (ROM 0x8ECC0),
lrg_rev1 0x80088B70 (ROM 0x89770). All three bodies were read and are
identical to the USA body apart from the four relocated call targets; all
three verify byte-exact from the same source.

## Recovered behaviour

Interrupts are masked, and the mask in force beforehand is remembered.

The routine then walks the list of blocks hanging off the anchor. For each
block it first notes the successor, because the block may be moved during the
step. If the block's owner tag equals the tag passed in, the block is being
reclaimed: its size is folded into the successor's size when a successor
exists, the block is unlinked from the list it is on, and it is inserted at the
head of the anchor's own list. Blocks whose tag does not match are left alone.
The walk continues from the successor noted at the top of the step, so a block
that was moved does not disturb the traversal.

When the list is exhausted the previous interrupt mask is restored. Nothing is
returned.

## Status

Exact. Byte-identical in USA, Japan, Europe and lrg_rev1.
