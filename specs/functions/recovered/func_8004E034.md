# func_8004E034

## Boundary
* USA VRAM 0x8004E034 - 0x8004E09F (108 bytes, 27 instructions), ROM 0x4EC34.
* Frame of 0x28 bytes; the return address is saved at the usual slot and the
  body ends with a return plus an empty delay slot.
* Five direct callers target 0x8004E034. Boundary confirmed, not corrected.

## Callers / callees
* Callers: 5.
* Callees: `func_80014CC0`, called once. That helper takes an angle in degrees
  as its first (floating-point) argument and two pointers to single-precision
  outputs; it is the game's degree sine/cosine pair.

## Parameters
Two single-precision floats, arriving in the two floating-point argument
registers and spilled to their caller-provided home slots on entry (they must
survive the call). No globals are referenced.

## Frame layout established from the original
* two 4-byte floating-point locals at frame offsets 0x18 and 0x1C, whose
  addresses are handed to the helper as the second and third arguments;
* eight further bytes of local space at 0x20-0x27 that the body never reads or
  writes.

## Recovered behaviour
The function converts an arc length into the height of the circular segment it
subtends. It divides the first argument (an arc length) by the second (a radius)
and scales the ratio by 180, producing an angle in degrees; the game's degree
trigonometry helper turns that angle into a sine and a cosine, of which only the
cosine is used. The result is one minus that cosine, halved, and multiplied back
by the radius - the standard "sagitta" expression. The sine output is requested
but discarded.

## Compiler evidence

The cosine output is read through its volatile-qualified local before the
height expression is evaluated. Separating the division result from the final
radius multiplication preserves the original floating-point lifetimes and its
real division by `2.0f`; both choices express the behavior directly.

## Status
Byte-matching C. IDO 5.3 with the accepted `ido53_o2` profile reproduces all
108 bytes exactly for USA retail and the LRG USA revision.
