# func_8004E034

## Boundary
* USA VRAM 0x8004E034 - 0x8004E09F (108 bytes, 27 instructions), ROM 0x4EC34.
* Frame of 0x28 bytes; the return address is saved at the usual slot and the
  body ends with a return plus an empty delay slot.
* Five direct callers target 0x8004E034. Boundary confirmed, not corrected.

## Callers / callees
* Callers: 5.
* Callees: `func_80014CC0`, called once. That helper takes an angle in degrees
  as its first (floating-point) argument and two pointers to single-precision
  outputs; it is the game's degree sine/cosine pair.

## Parameters
Two single-precision floats, arriving in the two floating-point argument
registers and spilled to their caller-provided home slots on entry (they must
survive the call). No globals are referenced.

## Frame layout established from the original
* two 4-byte floating-point locals at frame offsets 0x18 and 0x1C, whose
  addresses are handed to the helper as the second and third arguments;
* eight further bytes of local space at 0x20-0x27 that the body never reads or
  writes.

yet exact.

## Recovered behaviour
The function converts an arc length into the height of the circular segment it
subtends. It divides the first argument (an arc length) by the second (a radius)
and scales the ratio by 180, producing an angle in degrees; the game's degree
trigonometry helper turns that angle into a sine and a cosine, of which only the
cosine is used. The result is one minus that cosine, halved, and multiplied back
by the radius - the standard "sagitta" expression. The sine output is requested
but discarded.

## Compiler evidence gathered
* The division by two survives as a real floating-point division. Under the
  accepted `-O2` profile the compiler rewrites a division by a power of two into
  a multiplication by its reciprocal, which the original body does not contain;
  under `-O1` the division is preserved. Everything else about the `-O1` build
  also lines up, so this translation unit was very probably built at `-O1`.
* The eight unused bytes of local space are reproduced by any eight bytes of
  declared-but-unread locals placed before the two outputs (an unused 8-byte
  local, or two unused 4-byte ones). With that padding and `-O1` the build is
  108 bytes, and the prologue, the call setup, the whole instruction sequence and
  the epilogue all agree with the original.
* What still differs is only the numbering of six floating-point temporaries in
  the tail expression: the original loads the cosine into a temporary before
  materialising the constant one, while every source shape tried here emits the
  constant first. Twelve expression shapes (operand order, parenthesisation,
  negation, extra named temporaries, pointer indirection, nested block) and the
  flag axes `-O1`/`-O2`/`-g1`/`-g3`, `-mips1`/`-mips2`/`-mips3 -o32`, IDO 5.3 and
  IDO 7.1 all produce the same allocation, so the remaining difference is not
  reachable by rewriting the expression.
* Because the padding local is a fabrication that cannot be justified from
  behaviour, the committed source is the clean two-local form. A future worker
  who wants the near-match starting point can find the padded variants in
  `probes/v4E034/`.

## Status
Behaviour. Reviewed C, behaviour-correct, not byte-identical; the instruction
