## Recovered behavior

A byte-granular copy. Given a source address, a destination address and a signed
length, it copies that many bytes from the source to the destination in
ascending order and returns nothing. A length of zero or less copies nothing.
There is no overlap handling and no alignment requirement: the copy is
byte-at-a-time regardless of how the two addresses line up.

The compiled form is the compiler's own four-way unrolling of that single loop:
the remainder (length modulo four) is copied first one byte per pass, then the
rest four bytes per pass. The unrolling is automatic, so the specification is
the plain loop.

Two shape details matter. The two addresses are walked with their own advancing
cursors rather than being indexed from a base, and those cursors are separate
locals initialised from the parameters rather than the parameters themselves;
advancing the parameters directly saves the two register copies at entry and
comes out eight bytes short, while indexing from the base rearranges the whole
prologue.
