# func_8008FE60 — push a record onto a list with interrupts masked

## Boundary

* USA VRAM `0x8008FE60`–`0x8008FEAF`, ROM `0x90A60`, 80 bytes.
* Frame is 0x18 bytes; both parameters are kept in their caller-provided home
  slots across the two calls. Ends with the standard return sequence and an
  empty delay slot.
* Single direct caller targets this entry; no branch from the preceding
  function reaches into it. Boundary as assigned is correct.

## Callers

One direct call site in USA, inside the routine at `0x800891xx` that has just
initialised the record it is about to hand over: it zeroes the record's link
word, stores a handler address into the record, points the record's second
word at itself, and stores the list owner into the record before calling.

## Callees

* `func_80090500` — the interrupt-mask primitive. Called with the value 1 to
  mask interrupts, returning the previous mask, and called again with that
  saved mask to restore it.

## Globals

None referenced directly.

## Access widths

All accesses are 32-bit words:

* owner offset 0x00: head-of-list pointer, read then rewritten.
* owner offset 0x20: a word copied into the new record.
* record offset 0x00: link to the previous head.
* record offset 0x10: destination of the copied owner word.

## Recovered behavior

The routine links a record into a singly linked list owned by its first
argument, doing so inside a critical section. It first masks interrupts and
remembers the previous mask. It copies one word from the owner (offset 0x20)
into the record (offset 0x10) — the record inherits that setting from its
owner. It then performs the usual head insertion: the record's link word takes
the current list head, and the owner's head word is repointed at the record.
Finally the previous interrupt mask is restored. Nothing is returned.

## Status

Exact in USA, Japan, Europe and lrg_rev1 under the accepted IDO 5.3 -O2
profile.
