# func_80075648

## Verified facts

- USA VRAM `0x80075648` through `0x8007593F`, ROM `0x76248`, 760 bytes.
  The body ends at `jr ra`; its delay slot restores the 160-byte stack frame.
- Thirteen distinct USA call instructions target the entry. The function calls
  `func_80015288`, `func_800154D0`, `func_80015538`, `func_800155C0`,
  `func_800156DC`, `func_80017874`, `func_80017BA8`, `func_800181BC`,
  `func_8003FA24`, `func_800735BC`, `func_80073640`, `func_800736AC`,
  `func_800755FC`, and `func_80082BE0`.
- The addressed globals are `D_800AD9B8`, `D_800AD9BC`, `D_8011C7A0`,
  `D_8011C7A4`, and the matrix at `D_8011C7A8`.
- Unique normalized mappings place the structural equivalents at Japan
  `0x80076278` / ROM `0x76E78` and Europe `0x8007AB28` / ROM `0x7B728`.
  LRG uses the USA address.

## Recovered behavior

When both global handles are active and the second handle passes its validation
helper, the routine records the supplied index on the object and initializes a
temporary transform. It fills two three-component axes from signed randomized
values bounded by two global floats, derives the third axis with cross products,
normalizes the basis, and independently scales all three axes by randomized
factors derived from the caller's amount.

The translation row comes from the object's indexed position table when the
index is nonnegative, otherwise from the fallback vector. The routine then
selects an attached identifier or the object's fallback identifier, combines
the temporary transform with the shared matrix, submits it to the active
handle, and applies the final handle configuration.

The two four-byte tag values passed before initialization are retained as
uninterpreted identifiers; their semantic names are not yet established.

## Compiler status

Behavior recovered, not byte-matching. IDO 5.3 `-O2` emits the canonical
760-byte extent. Placing `tag` in the validated inner scope reproduces its
canonical stack offset `0x48`; the previous outer scope used `0x58`.

Four instruction words still differ at offsets `0x7C`, `0x80`, `0x8C`, and
`0x90`. The original loads the conversion denominator, base, and range in that
order, while the current compiler schedules range, base, then denominator.
The isolated exact verifier therefore reports first differing byte 126. No
byte-match claim is made.
